**Surface, colour & lighting**

```text
esports team and tournament graphics a pro team or game streamer would publish: matchday poster, roster or result post, channel banner, stream screen, team badge. GRAPHIC LANGUAGE: flat angular panels all cut at one shared slash angle of about 15 degrees, outer corners chamfered; a solid title panel carries the headline and a slim info bar beneath it carries the match or channel details; a tight group of three chevrons and one thin band of diagonal hazard stripes point one way; small corner brackets and tiny metadata text; optionally a row of empty sponsor boxes along one edge. LIGHT: a dark arena - hard white rim light from behind and above the subject, a soft team-coloured wash from an off-frame LED wall, light haze crossed by two or three narrow stage beams; edges brighter than the centre. COLOUR: near-black or deep navy over most of the frame; one bold team colour as a large flat panel field; one high-saturation accent (acid lime, electric cyan, hot magenta or signal orange) in one or two small spots; white only for small text; no rainbow gradients. TEXTURE: fine film grain, halftone dot fades along panel edges and faint scanlines; panels read as flat printed shapes, not glossy 3D objects. TIERS: streamer tier is darker with a thin accent glow along panel edges; pro tier floods larger areas with the team colour, drops the glow and shows more grain. No real game, league or team logos, no game characters, no player likenesses. LETTERFORM COLOR PRIORITY: if the selected font defines its own letterform colour or material, that takes absolute priority; the team palette colours the letterforms only when the font does not specify. ATMOSPHERE: the team colour and accent always appear in panels, rim light, LED wash and haze regardless of letterform colour. When no specific material is defined by the font: default lighting - the headline sits on the flat title panel under hard white rim light, lifted by a thin dark drop shadow, in a dark arena of team-coloured haze.
```

## Tips

- Short, aggressive team names, gamer tags, or single impact words in full capitals work best with this style.
- Swap the electric cyan accent for acid lime, signal orange, or hot magenta to completely change the team's visual identity.
- Short words (1–3 words, under ~15 letters) come out most reliably on general models; long phrases are where spelling breaks first.
- Results are best on FontVibe, where this style is paired with a real font, a font reference image and the model it was tuned on. General models get the material, colour and mood but choose their own letter shapes.
