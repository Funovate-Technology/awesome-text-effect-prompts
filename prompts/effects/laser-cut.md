## Original FontVibe style layer

This is the style layer FontVibe itself uses. It is **not** a complete prompt: on FontVibe it is combined with a font description, a font reference image and the model routing for that style, so some sentences refer to "the chosen font". Use the standalone prompt above with other tools.

**Surface, colour & lighting**

```text
Precision fabrication aesthetic: text exists as physical cut material — not rendered, not painted. The laser kerf is the only boundary: mathematically exact edges, zero roughness.

MATERIAL SURFACE RULES:
— Wood: warm grain on letter face (birch honey or dark walnut); charred dark seam along every kerf edge; cross-grain cross-section at cut face. Gentle warm transmission through thin sections.
— Acrylic: glassy-smooth kerf edges scatter light as luminous halos — frosted emits soft white-blue edge glow; clear creates prismatic edge-light. Face stays matte or polished.
— Metal: bright machined cut face against brushed exterior. Raking light reveals mechanical precision and directional grain.
— Paper/Organic: bone-clean cut edges casting hairline shadows on backing. Maximum filigree detail preserved.

FABRICATION LAWS (all materials):
— Enclosed counters (O,D,B,P,R,A) retained by structural bridge connections — thin material tabs turned into design features.
— Material thickness always visible as dimensional depth: each letter is a physical slab.
— Negative space is real air or backing material showing through.
— No added outlines, glow effects, or drop shadows — the kerf edge is the only boundary.

FORBIDDEN: graphic fills, smooth gradient fills on letter faces, extruded 3D depth.

When no specific material is defined by the font: default lighting — warm amber side light raking from low-left, deep warm shadow on right and behind, dark warm-brown craft-studio background.
```

**Scene**

```text
Dark raw-timber workshop surface or dark slate slab beneath the cut piece, background in near-black with warm craft-studio atmosphere. Directional key light from upper-left at a shallow rake angle — casting fine sharp shadow lines along every kerf edge and revealing the material slab depth. Warm amber secondary fill from low right. The cut piece presented flat, just released from the machine bed: clean, exact, nothing to add.
```

## Tips

- Words with enclosed loops (like O, B, P, or R) will naturally highlight the authentic look of the laser-cut bridge tabs.
- To vary the aesthetic, replace the wood description with frosted acrylic featuring glowing light-scattering edges, or brushed metal with bright machined cut faces.
- Short words (1–3 words, under ~15 letters) come out most reliably on general models; long phrases are where spelling breaks first.
- Results are best on FontVibe, where this style is paired with a real font, a font reference image and the model it was tuned on. General models get the material, colour and mood but choose their own letter shapes.
