**Surface, colour & lighting**

```text
Intimate writing-desk light field — warm directional light arriving from a 30–45-degree upper-left angle, the quality of morning window light filtering through gauze or the halo of a brass desk lamp; this raking light travels across the paper surface at low angles, revealing fiber tooth and ink body depth simultaneously. Secondary warm fill from the right side at 20–25% key intensity preserves intimate shadow without eliminating it. The substrate is always textured paper: hot-press cotton rag, aged parchment, smooth vellum, cream laid stock, or cold-press watercolor paper — paper surface grain and fiber are visibly revealed under raking light, with a subtle warmth yellowing toward the edges. Ink sits proud on the surface: a subtle wet sheen at maximum-density stroke points, matte dry finish at hairlines; at dramatic stroke termini, micro-droplets of ink may cling to paper fiber. Atmospheric details: floating paper dust motes caught in window light rays, faint ink bloom halos around dense stroke endpoints. Color temperature: warm amber-sepia throughout the background environment and paper surface; slightly cooler clear-white at the central text zone for luminous contrast. Background beyond the writing surface: soft warm bokeh or warm shadow — never competing with the foreground. Letter surface color and material are defined entirely by each font's own prompt_core — this style provides only the writing-desk light field, paper substrate environment, and ink-on-surface atmosphere.

When no specific material is defined by the font: morning window light from upper-left at 35 degrees on warm cream cotton laid paper, paper fiber texture clearly visible under raking light, soft amber fill from the right at 25% intensity.
```

## Tips

- Short poetic phrases, formal names, dates, or correspondence sign-offs complement this intimate letter aesthetic best.
- To alter the mood, specify a different paper substrate such as rough cold-press watercolor sheet or smooth aged vellum.
- Short words (1–3 words, under ~15 letters) come out most reliably on general models; long phrases are where spelling breaks first.
- Results are best on FontVibe, where this style is paired with a real font, a font reference image and the model it was tuned on. General models get the material, colour and mood but choose their own letter shapes.
