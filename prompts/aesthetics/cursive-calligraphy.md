This is the style layer FontVibe itself uses. It is **not** a complete prompt: on FontVibe it is combined with a font description, a font reference image and the model routing for that style, so some sentences refer to "the chosen font". Use the standalone prompt above with other tools.

**Surface, colour & lighting**

```text
laid down as the exacting art of formal pointed-pen calligraphy. SURFACE / INK: crisp, controlled ink — deep black or warm sepia — sitting cleanly on the page with fine wet-ink edges and a faint natural sheen where it catches the light, precise and disciplined rather than loose, the exacting hand of a master penman. GROUND: fine calligraphy paper — smooth hot-press cotton, cream or ivory practice stock, sometimes with faint ruled guideline slants, or a formal certificate or invitation ground — with generous margins so the lettering reads. LIGHT: even, clean studio light — soft and flat — showing the crisp ink edges and fine wet-ink detail, minimal drama. ATMOSPHERE: formal, timeless and disciplined — the tradition of copperplate and Spencerian penmanship, wedding invitations, diplomas and certificates, the poised craft of the calligrapher's desk. LETTERFORM PRIORITY: when the chosen font carries its own treatment, that font's letterform shapes and built-in flourishes take priority and own the letters; this style governs only the ink surface, ground and light, never the letter shapes. When no specific material is defined by the font: crisp controlled pointed-pen ink with fine wet edges and a faint sheen, in a single deep tone, on the calligraphy-paper ground. COLOR: if the font declares its own color keep it; otherwise the ink reads as a classic penmanship tone — deep black or warm sepia — on the pale paper ground.
```

## Tips

- Words associated with formal occasions, names, or elegant single nouns work best with this disciplined penmanship aesthetic.
- Switch between deep carbon black and warm sepia ink tones to adjust the vintage feel of the document.
- Short words (1–3 words, under ~15 letters) come out most reliably on general models; long phrases are where spelling breaks first.
- Results are best on FontVibe, where this style is paired with a real font, a font reference image and the model it was tuned on. General models get the material, colour and mood but choose their own letter shapes.
