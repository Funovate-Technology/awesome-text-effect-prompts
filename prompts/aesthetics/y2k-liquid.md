**Surface, colour & lighting**

```text
Y2K liquid chrome surface — mirror-polished metallic liquid caught mid-pour, frozen in time. Surface shows viscous flow lines and mercury-like surface tension. Chrome liquid drips extend from letter bases, tapered to fine suspended points, still stretching under gravity. Color: cool silver base with shifting pink-blue-lavender iridescent reflections across the curved liquid surface — the Y2K-era chrome fantasy, not realistic mercury. Soft gradient environment light (pale pink, ice blue, silver white) bounces off the liquid surfaces, creating color-shifting highlights. Background: light-to-mid-tone gradient — soft silver-white, pale pink, or lavender mist — never pure black, which kills the iridescent color reflections. Photorealistic liquid metal rendering. LETTERFORM PRIORITY: when the chosen font carries its own treatment, that font's letterform shapes take priority and own the letters; this style governs the liquid-chrome material, the viscous flow and drips and the iridescent reflections. FALLBACK MATERIAL: if the font carries no material of its own, render the letters as mirror-polished liquid-chrome letters caught mid-pour with tapered chrome drips and pink-blue-lavender iridescence on a soft light gradient (never pure black). COLOR PRIORITY: keep the font's own colors if specified; otherwise a cool silver base with shifting pink-blue-lavender iridescent reflections.
```

**Scene**

```text
Soft silver-white studio gradient background, two-point colored environment lighting: warm pale pink from left, cool ice-blue from right. Letter surfaces catch both colors in shifting iridescent bands across the liquid chrome. Chrome drip points hang below letter bases, catching the pink-blue light split. Minimal, clean, maximum liquid material focus.
```

## Tips

- Short words or single-word titles work best, leaving enough negative space for the suspended drips and liquid tension to read cleanly.
- You can vary the mood by changing the two lighting accents to other pastel pairs, such as mint green and peach, while keeping the background light.
- Short words (1–3 words, under ~15 letters) come out most reliably on general models; long phrases are where spelling breaks first.
- Results are best on FontVibe, where this style is paired with a real font, a font reference image and the model it was tuned on. General models get the material, colour and mood but choose their own letter shapes.
