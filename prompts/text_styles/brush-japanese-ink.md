# Japanese Ink Brush

**Surface, colour & lighting**

```text
Japanese ink brush aesthetic — the visual language of sumi calligraphy on washi paper.

INK MEDIUM: sumi pigment in deep black to charcoal range — richest density at mark centers, naturally transitioning outward as ink supply depletes. Fully matte, absorbed into paper surface, producing a tonal range from dense opaque black to transparent grey depending on dilution and brush load.

PAPER SURFACE: natural washi — warm cream-white base with visible paper fiber structure giving slight texture. The absorbent surface creates the characteristic soft border quality of East Asian brush work at all mark edges.

LIGHT ATMOSPHERE: soft diffuse north-facing studio light — the traditional calligrapher's light source. Even, shadowless, allowing the full tonal range of ink marks to read clearly. Warm cream paper tone contrasts against deep sumi black.

AESTHETIC REGISTER: the ma principle — the space between and around marks is as considered as the marks themselves. Compositions have breathing room and deliberate quietness.

When no specific material is defined by the font: default environment — deep sumi black marks on warm cream washi paper, soft diffuse overhead light, meditative studio atmosphere.
```

**Scene**

```text
Text fills the frame, large and centered on natural washi paper surface. Soft diffuse north-facing studio light from above, even and shadowless. Sparse composition with generous white space — the quietness of a Japanese calligraphy studio.
```

## Tips

- Short single words or succinct terms work best, preserving the generous negative space essential to this style.
- To adjust the mood, specify whether you want predominantly dense, saturated black ink marks or softer, more diluted grey washes.
- Short words (1–3 words, under ~15 letters) come out most reliably on general models; long phrases are where spelling breaks first.
- Results are best on FontVibe, where this style is paired with a real font, a font reference image and the model it was tuned on. General models get the material, colour and mood but choose their own letter shapes.
